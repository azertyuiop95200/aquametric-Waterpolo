WATER POLO KNOWLEDGE

Classic water-polo tactics, synthesized

A practical tactical library: what each structure tries to create, what the players must read, and the usual counter. Source material stays internal to AquaMetric; this page shows the synthesis.

AquaMetric tactical synthesis

These are reference structures, not automatic instructions for a specific opponent. Match-specific recommendations belong in scouting and match analysis.

Defence

Classic defensive structures

PRESS

Press defence

Defenders stay tight to perimeter attackers to deny clean catches and force play away from preferred hands. It works best with strong legs, communication and reliable help behind the press.

Read: ball pressure, passing lanes and the centre matchup.
FRONT

Press + front the centre

The centre defender takes the front line while perimeter defenders pressure the ball so the entry pass becomes difficult. Weak-side help must be ready if the ball is reversed quickly.

Counter: fast reversal, drive behind the front or a high-quality lob entry.
DROP

Drop / slough defence

One or more perimeter defenders sink toward two metres to protect the centre and invite a lower-value outside shot. The defence must recover on the pass and avoid giving away uncontested specialist shooting.

Counter: move the ball before the drop settles and punish the open shooter.
2–4 / M

2–4 drop / M-zone hybrid

A compact hybrid protects the central lane and centre while preserving enough pressure on key shooters. Responsibilities change with ball position, so communication matters more than the diagram itself.

Read: who is allowed to receive, who drops and where the goalkeeper wants the block.
Attack

Classic attacking structures

2 METRES

Centre attack at 2 m

The offence creates a passing window to the centre through sealing, position changes and timely entries. The objective can be a direct finish, an exclusion or a collapse that frees the perimeter.

Key detail: entry angle and timing matter more than simply forcing the ball inside.
MOVEMENT

Drives, picks and perimeter movement

Crosses, screens and timed drives disturb matchups and create a temporary shooting or entry lane. Good movement is coordinated with the ball; random swimming only compresses the attack.

Read: defender switches, late help and the space created after the pick.
Transition

Transition structures

COUNTER

Counterattack and secondary break

The first objective is numerical or positional superiority before the defence is organised. If the direct counter is closed, the secondary break must flow immediately into structured offence instead of stopping.

Track: first three strokes, outlet quality, lane occupation and decision speed.
RECOVERY

Defensive recovery

After losing possession, players protect the central lane first, identify the most dangerous attacker and communicate matchups while swimming back. A fast organised recovery can cancel a numerical disadvantage.

Priority: goal-side position before perfect individual matchups.
Special teams

Zone+ and Zone−

ZONE+ · 4–2

Zone+ 4–2

Four players form the outside line with two close to goal. The attack seeks rapid ball movement, a clean post connection or a shot created by moving the goalkeeper and blocking line.

Read: post defender depth, blocker hand and goalkeeper movement.
ZONE+ · 3–3

Zone+ 3–3 / rotating shape

Three higher and three lower positions can rotate to change passing angles and stress the compact five-player defence. The value comes from timing and role clarity, not from constant motion.

Counter-read: identify whether the defence protects the posts, centre lane or primary shooter.
ZONE− · 5v6

Zone− compact 5 v 6

The five defenders protect the highest-value lanes, coordinate blocks with the goalkeeper and shift as one unit with the ball. Good penalty killing accepts some low-value shots while removing post and central finishes.

Track: block lane, goalkeeper sight line, post control and rebound responsibility.
